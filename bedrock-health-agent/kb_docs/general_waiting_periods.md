# General knowledge: Waiting periods and pre-existing conditions

> Simplified, fictionalised explanations for demo purposes. Not financial or
> regulatory advice — a licensed human should confirm anything regulatory.

## What is a waiting period?
A waiting period is the time you must hold a policy before you can claim for a
particular service. It stops people from joining only when they already need a
big procedure.

## Common maximum waiting periods (typical)
- **2 months:** most general hospital services and most general extras.
- **6 months:** many major extras (e.g. major dental).
- **12 months:** pre-existing conditions, pregnancy and birth, and certain
  clinical categories such as joint reconstruction or psychiatric (restricted).

## Pre-existing conditions
A pre-existing condition is an ailment, illness or condition where signs or
symptoms existed in the 6 months before you joined or upgraded — as assessed by
a medical practitioner appointed by the fund. These generally carry a **12-month**
waiting period before you can claim for related hospital treatment.

## Switching funds
If you switch to an equivalent level of cover, you generally **do not** re-serve
waiting periods you have already fully served. You may still need to serve
waiting periods for any *higher* level of cover or *new* services your old policy
didn't include. This is why a "switcher" should compare like-for-like coverage,
not just price.

## Restricted vs full cover
"Restricted" cover pays benefits only at the default (public) rate, which can
leave large out-of-pocket costs if treated as a private patient. Always check
whether a clinical category is fully covered or restricted.

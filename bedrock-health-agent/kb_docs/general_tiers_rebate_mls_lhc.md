# General knowledge: Tiers, Rebate, MLS and LHC

> Simplified, fictionalised explanations for demo purposes. Not financial or
> regulatory advice — a licensed human should confirm anything regulatory.

## Product tiers (Basic / Bronze / Silver / Gold)
Australian hospital cover is grouped into four standard tiers. Higher tiers
must cover more clinical categories:
- **Basic:** minimum cover, mostly restricted categories. Cheapest.
- **Bronze:** covers many common, lower-cost procedures.
- **Silver:** adds bigger-ticket items like joint reconstruction and cataracts.
- **Gold:** must cover everything, including pregnancy, joint replacement and
  comprehensive psychiatric.
"Plus" versions (e.g. Silver Plus) add extra categories above the tier minimum.

## Extras cover
Extras (a.k.a. "ancillary") cover everyday out-of-hospital services like dental,
optical, physio and psychology, usually with annual dollar limits per service.

## Private Health Insurance Rebate
A government rebate that reduces your premium, income-tested. Lower incomes get a
larger percentage rebate; higher incomes get less or nothing. The rebate
percentage also steps up slightly for older age brackets.

## Medicare Levy Surcharge (MLS)
An extra tax (on top of the normal 2% Medicare Levy) for higher-income earners
who do NOT hold an eligible level of private hospital cover. Holding qualifying
hospital cover lets you avoid the MLS.

## Lifetime Health Cover (LHC) loading
If you first take out hospital cover after your "LHC base day" (around age 31),
you pay a loading of 2% extra per year of age, up to a maximum of 70%. The
loading is removed after you've held cover continuously for 10 years. This is why
younger people are encouraged to take out hospital cover earlier.
